Lecture 25

The Riemann integral and uniform continuity

We define an integral by lower and upper estimates on a finite interval. Their difference gives a criterion for integrability. We prove that monotone functions satisfy it, then use subsequences to obtain uniform continuity for continuous functions on a closed interval.

Uses.

  • Summary 1: The number 2\sqrt 22​ is irrational.

  • Summary 2: Induction proves the finite-square-sum identity. Density supplies rationals in every real interval. The Archimedean property supplies fine meshes. R supplies infima and suprema of nonempty bounded real sets. Absolute values satisfy the triangle and reverse triangle inequalities.

  • Summary 3: The real limit laws control finite algebraic expressions.

  • Summary 4: Eventual order passes to limits. The squeeze theorem proves convergence. Every bounded real sequence has a convergent subsequence; closed-interval bounds survive its limit.

  • Summary 5: Continuity is relative to the domain. A continuous function preserves the limit of every approaching domain sequence.

25.3 Monotone functions and a failure of integrability

Proof. Suppose first that fff is nondecreasing. Its endpoint values bound it. For the uniform partition into nnn intervals, the endpoints give the infimum and supremum on each subinterval. Summary

  • Definitions 25.1 and 25.2 and Theorem 25.3: a bounded real function on [a,b][a,b][a,b], a<ba<ba<b, is integrable exactly when its Darboux gap can be made arbitrarily small.
  • Theorem 25.5: every monotone real function on [a,b][a,b][a,b] is integrable. The bounded Dirichlet function is not integrable.
  • Theorem 25.6 and Equation (25.2): integrals are linear, order-preserving and additive over adjacent intervals; ∣∫f∣≤∫∣f∣|\int f|\leq\int|f|∣∫f∣≤∫∣f∣.
  • Definition 25.7 and Theorem 25.8: a continuous real function on a closed finite interval is uniformly continuous. The proof uses Bolzano–Weierstrass from Lecture 4.

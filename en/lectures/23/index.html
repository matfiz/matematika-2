Lecture 23

Antiderivatives and integration rules

What this lecture does

An antiderivative reverses differentiation on a specified interval. Its ambiguity is one additive constant on that interval. The chain rule gives substitution, and the product rule gives integration by parts. Every formula we obtain can be verified by differentiating it on the original domain.

23.4 Integration by parts from the product rule

Theorem 23.6 (Antiderivative integration by parts). Let u,v:I→Ru,v:I\to\Ru,v:I→R be differentiable on a nonempty open interval. Proof. The product rule gives (uv)′=u′v+uv′(uv)'=u'v+uv'(uv)′=u′v+uv′. If H′=u′vH'=u'vH′=u′v, subtraction gives (uv−H)′=uv′(uv-H)'=uv'(uv−H)′=uv′. Conversely, if G′=uv′G'=uv'G′=uv′, the same product rule gives (uv−G)′=u′v(uv-G)'=u'v(uv−G)′=u′v. This proves the converse existence statement. No continuity of the derivatives was used; the differentiability and stated primitive hypotheses supply all required steps. 

To apply the theorem, choose a factor uuu and a remaining factor v′v'v′. Find a primitive vvv of that remaining factor. Differentiate uuu, multiply it by vvv and find a primitive HHH of u′vu'vu′v. The answer is then uv−H+Cuv-H+Cuv−H+C. The product term and the minus sign follow from the product rule, so they must be retained even when the second primitive is simple. This rule is called integration by parts. It replaces one primitive problem by another; the choice is useful when the new integrand is easier to handle.

Summary

  • Theorem 23.1: once one primitive is known on a nonempty open interval, all are F+CF+CF+C. Separate domain intervals have independent constants. A value at one point fixes that interval’s constant.
  • §23.2: the table reverses the known derivative formulas on their stated domains. Real-power, logarithm and trigonometric restrictions remain part of each answer.
  • Theorem 23.2: for differentiable g:I→Jg:I\to Jg:I→J and a primitive FFF of fff on JJJ, the primitive family of f(g)g′f(g)g'f(g)g′ on III is F∘g+CF\circ g+CF∘g+C. Monotonicity and a nonzero inner derivative are not required.
  • Theorem 23.6: if H′=u′vH'=u'vH′=u′v for differentiable uuu and vvv, the primitives of uv′uv'uv′ are uv−H+Cuv-H+Cuv−H+C on the same interval. Every proposed answer can be tested by differentiating on its original domain.

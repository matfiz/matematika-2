Lecture 24

Integration techniques

Integration rules become useful by matching the integrand’s structure. Parts handles products, partial fractions separates rational functions, and trigonometric identities or a specified inverse branch handle powers and square roots. Polynomial factorisation and the square-matrix criterion justify the partial-fraction decomposition.

Uses.

  • Summary 8: The product, chain and inverse differentiation rules hold on their stated intervals. The derivatives of logarithms and inverse trigonometric functions are known.

  • Summary 16: Real polynomials divide with remainder and factor into real linear and irreducible quadratic factors, using F. Nonzero polynomials have finitely many roots; real quadratic nonreal roots occur in conjugate pairs.

  • Summary 19: A linear map on real coordinate columns has a unique standard matrix.

  • Summary 20: A square coefficient matrix with zero null space is invertible and solves every right side uniquely.

  • Summary 23: All primitives on an interval differ by a constant. Substitution retains the inner derivative. The product rule gives integration by parts, including its primitive-existence hypotheses.

Summary

  • Parts differentiates one selected factor and integrates the other. Repeated polynomial reduction and cyclic product identities give the stated patterns.
  • Theorem 24.1: after polynomial division and real factorisation, every proper rational function has the stated unique partial fractions. The proof uses F through factorisation and the square coefficient-system criterion.
  • Equation (24.1): linear powers and completed-square quadratic terms integrate by the logarithm, power and arctangent rules. The proved recurrence supplies every repeated quadratic power.
  • Odd trigonometric powers can supply a substitution factor; even powers use the addition identities. A radical substitution retains its inverse branch and the sign of the square root.
  • All formulas describe primitive families on their original intervals. Differentiate the final answer and retain independent constants across actual domain breaks.

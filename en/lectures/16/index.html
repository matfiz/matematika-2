Lecture 16

Complex exponentials and real polynomials

What this lecture does

Complex exponential notation records the polar multiplication law in one formula. We use complex roots to factor real polynomials into real linear and quadratic factors. Differentiation with respect to a real parameter then relates that notation to real oscillations and circular motion. The existence of complex polynomial roots is stated as a standing assumption.

16.1 Complex exponential notation

16.2 Polynomial division and multiplicity

A complex polynomial is a finite expression P(z)=a0+a1z+⋯+anznP(z)=a_0+a_1z+\cdots+a_nz^nP(z)=a0​+a1​z+⋯+an​zn with complex coefficients. This follows because the highest term is the product of their nonzero leading coefficients, and no other term has that degree.

16.3 Complex roots and real factors

Standing fact F: fundamental theorem of algebra.

Every nonconstant complex polynomial has at least one complex root. We assume this existence theorem.

Combining F with the factor theorem gives a full factorisation of every nonzero degree-nnn complex polynomial: P(z)=an∏j=1n(z−αj).P(z)=a_n\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-\alpha_j).P(z)=an​j=1∏n​(z−αj​). Here the finite product multiplies the factors with indices j=1,…,nj=1,\ldots,nj=1,…,n. Repeated roots occur repeatedly in the list. To prove the formula, use F to find one root, divide out its linear factor, and apply F again to each positive-degree quotient. Each step lowers the degree by one. After nnn steps the quotient is constant, equal to the original leading coefficient because all removed factors are monic, meaning leading coefficient 1. For n=0n=0n=0, the product is empty and is defined to be 1. If a root α\alphaα occurs kkk times in this list, collect those factors to get P(z)=(z−α)kQ(z)P(z)=(z-\alpha)^kQ(z)P(z)=(z−α)kQ(z). Theorem 16.3 (Real factorisation from F). Every nonzero real polynomial is a nonzero real constant times a product of real linear factors and real quadratic factors with no real roots. The factors may repeat.

Proof. Use the complete complex factorisation just proved from F. Every real root supplies a real linear factor. Pair each nonreal root α=a+ib\alpha=a+\ii bα=a+ib, with b≠0b\ne0b=0, with its conjugate, using their equal multiplicities. The paired factors multiply to (z−α)(z−α‾)=z2−2az+(a2+b2).(z-\alpha)(z-\conj\alpha)=z^2-2az+(a^2+b^2).(z−α)(z−α)=z2−2az+(a2+b2). These pairs and the real linear factors use all the complex factors. The leading coefficient is the required nonzero real constant. 

A real quadratic with no real root is irreducible over R\RR: it cannot be written as a product of two positive-degree real polynomials. Indeed, degrees would force two linear factors, each of which has a real root. The proof of Theorem 16.3 is a consequence of the explicit standing assumption F. It is not a proof of F itself.

Summary

  • Equation (16.1): the complex exponential is defined by real functions. It obeys the product law, never vanishes and has imaginary period 2πi2\pi\ii2πi. Equation (16.2) retains all argument representatives.
  • Theorem 16.1: polynomial division has a unique lower-degree remainder. Linear division gives the factor theorem and root multiplicity.
  • Standing fact F supplies a root to every nonconstant complex polynomial. Theorem 16.3 proves the resulting real linear/quadratic factorisation by pairing conjugates with equal multiplicity.
  • Equation (16.3): differentiation uses a real parameter. Phasors give sinusoidal amplitudes and phases; the zero motion has no assigned argument or least positive period.
  • A prescribed circle with R>0R>0R>0 and ω>0\omega>0ω>0 has tangential velocity iωq\ii\omega qiωq and inward acceleration −ω2q-\omega^2q−ω2q.

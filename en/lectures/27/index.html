Lecture 27

Improper integrals and the Gamma function

What this lecture does

An integral over an infinite interval, or past an unbounded endpoint, is a limit of finite integrals. We determine when that limit exists before computing it. Power models and comparison then justify an integral definition of the Gamma function and its recurrence.

27.1 Limits of finite integrals

Until now both endpoints were finite and the integrand was bounded. We keep the finite integral and take a limit of its endpoints. Throughout this lecture, every integrand is Riemann integrable on each closed finite subinterval that avoids its specified exceptional endpoints. All the examples below are continuous on those subintervals.

27.6 Functions defined by integrals

Summary

  • An improper integral is a finite limit of finite integrals. Every exceptional endpoint must be tested separately; cancellation of divergent pieces does not count as convergence.
  • The power x−px^{-p}x−p is integrable at infinity exactly for p>1p>1p>1, and at zero exactly for p<1p<1p<1. Nonnegative cutoff values converge exactly when bounded.
  • Theorems 27.4 and 27.7: positive comparison and positive finite limit comparison test convergence; absolute convergence implies convergence.
  • Equation (27.1) and Theorem 27.9: the Gamma integral converges exactly for s>0s>0s>0 and obeys Γ(s+1)=sΓ(s)\Gamma(s+1)=s\Gamma(s)Γ(s+1)=sΓ(s) and Γ(n+1)=n!\Gamma(n+1)=n!Γ(n+1)=n!.
  • Continuous integrands define differentiable accumulation functions, including erf⁡\operatorname{erf}erf and Si⁡\operatorname{Si}Si, even when their antiderivatives are not elementary [C].

Lecture 15

Complex arithmetic, roots and rotations

A multiplication on the coordinate plane produces a number whose square is −1-1−1. Moduli express distance conditions in that plane. The geometric meaning of multiplication by a nonzero number is rotation combined with a positive change of scale. Polar coordinates then turn the equation wn=zw^n=zwn=z into a length equation and an angle equation. A local two-by-two matrix calculation records the same multiplication for later linear algebra.

Uses.

  • Summary 2: Composition of maps and induction are defined. Standing fact E supplies the trigonometric identities and their usual periods.

  • Summary 3: The finite geometric sum follows by multiplication and cancellation.

  • Summary 4: Every positive real number has a unique positive nnnth root for an integer n≥1n\geq 1n≥1.

  • Summary 12: The Euclidean length in R2\R^2R2 is x2+y2\sqrt{x^2+y^2}x2+y2​ and obeys the triangle inequality.

  • Summary 14: Polar coordinates represent a nonzero planar point uniquely with positive radius and azimuth in [0,2π)[0,2\pi)[0,2π). Other arguments differ by full turns.

15.6 A real matrix representation

A real two-by-two matrix is an array with two rows and two columns. For this section, define addition and real scalar multiplication entrywise, and define multiplication by (pqrs)(uvwt)=(pu+qwpv+qtru+swrv+st).\begin{pmatrix}p&q\\r&s\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}u&v\\w&t\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}pu+qw&pv+qt\\ru+sw&rv+st\end{pmatrix}.(pr​qs​)(uw​vt​)=(pu+qwru+sw​pv+qtrv+st​). These local definitions will be generalised in Lecture 19. The matrix acts on an ordered pair (x,y)(x,y)(x,y) by giving the pair (px+qy,rx+sy)(px+qy,rx+sy)(px+qy,rx+sy). Distributing the expressions shows that the displayed matrix product acts by first applying the right matrix and then the left. Two matrices with the same action on every pair are equal, since their actions on (1,0)(1,0)(1,0) and (0,1)(0,1)(0,1) recover their columns. For three matrices, either grouping of their product acts on a pair by applying the third, then the second, then the first matrix. Associativity of function composition therefore proves associativity of this matrix product.

Define the map Φ\PhiΦ from C\CC to the set of real two-by-two matrices by Φ(a+ib)=(a−bba).\Phi(a+\ii b)=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\b&a\end{pmatrix}.Φ(a+ib)=(ab​−ba​). The first column recovers aaa and bbb, so the map is injective. Entrywise addition gives Φ(z+w)=Φ(z)+Φ(w)\Phi(z+w)=\Phi(z)+\Phi(w)Φ(z+w)=Φ(z)+Φ(w). For z=a+ibz=a+\ii bz=a+ib and w=c+idw=c+\ii dw=c+id, the local product gives Φ(z)Φ(w)=(ac−bd−(ad+bc)ad+bcac−bd)=Φ(zw).\Phi(z)\Phi(w) =\begin{pmatrix}ac-bd&-(ad+bc)\\ad+bc&ac-bd\end{pmatrix} =\Phi(zw).Φ(z)Φ(w)=(ac−bdad+bc​−(ad+bc)ac−bd​)=Φ(zw). Thus the map preserves multiplication as well. The identity and imaginary unit become Φ(1)=I2=(1001),Φ(i)=J=(0−110).\Phi(1)=I_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad \Phi(\ii)=J=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.Φ(1)=I2​=(10​01​),Φ(i)=J=(01​−10​). This representation relates complex arithmetic to composition of real coordinate transformations. It uses no determinant or matrix-inversion theorem.

Summary

  • Complex numbers are real pairs with the stated arithmetic. Conjugation gives zz‾=∣z∣2z\conj z=|z|^2zz=∣z∣2, and every nonzero number has reciprocal (15.1). Moduli multiply and obey the triangle inequality.
  • Polar multiplication adds arguments and multiplies moduli. Theorem 15.4: De Moivre’s formula holds for every integer exponent. Arguments of a nonzero number are defined modulo 2π2\pi2π.
  • Equation (15.2): a nonzero number has exactly nnn distinct nnnth roots for an integer n≥1n\geq1n≥1. Zero has only the root zero. Roots of unity sum to zero for n>1n>1n>1, and to 1 for n=1n=1n=1.
  • §15.6: the injective map Φ\PhiΦ preserves sums and products under the defined two-by-two matrix operations.
